## Intro

Brain organoids are pretty hot these days, mostly among brain researchers primarily using cellular and molecular techniques. "Mini-brains in a dish" has almost achieved meme status for them. There are some legitimate reasons for this and some less so. I thought I should put down some of them in writing as 2025 draws to a close.
Although only about 15 years old, the field has grown hugely. The flow-chart below shows my own big-picture map of the field.
{flow-chart}

Though their experimental approaches differ tremendously, people in each of these sub-fields are, in my humble opinion, fundamentally motivated by one overarching question. "How closely does a brain organoid mimic a similar-sized piece of intact human brain?" Over the last two years of experimental work, I tried to focus on a slightly different and narrower question:"how closely does a _cortical_ organoid of human origin approach the computational capabilities of a similar-sized piece of _human cortex_? I have to say I am far from any answer. I do wish the answer was "really close". Unfortunately, my guess is "not even close!". That said, I believe that even though the path to the ulitmate goal is currently hidden, we might learn many useful secrets of nature if we nevertheless keep at it for a bit.

My question is a minefield of unsolved issues. It begins starting with the phrase "computational capabilities". Serious arguments have been made regarding the 'computational' nature of the brain.
The brain had been studied for a many centuries before McCulloch and Pitts suggested (1) that it is a machine devoted to logic i.e. computation. Older metaphors compared the brain to a pump, pumping 'animal spirits' around the body (Descartes) (2). More recently, the brain, when controlling behavior, has been compared to a thermostat-like device mostly engaged in feedback control (perceptual control theory, William Powers / Henry Yin) comprising an heirarchy of control loops.This feedback control metaphor argues there is no need to invoke internal 'representations' which need to be computed on by brain circuits as assumed by the prevalent computational cognitive framework.This metaphor is so dominant that many young researchers might be unaware of the existence of other frameworks like perceptual control theory.

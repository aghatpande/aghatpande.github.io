## Intro

Brain organoids are pretty hot these days, mostly among cell and molecular brain researchers. "Mini-brains in a dish" has almost achieved meme status for these nerds. There are some legitimate reasons for this and some not so much. I thought I should put down some of them in writing as 2025 draws to a close.
Although only about 15 years old, the field has grown hugely. The flow-chart below shows my own big-picture map of the field.
{flow-chart}
Though their experimental approaches differ tremendously, people in each of these sub-fields are fundamentally tackling one major question, in my humble opinion. "How closely does a brain organoid mimic a similar-sized piece of intact human brain?" Over the last two years of experimental work, I tried to focus on a narrower question:"how closely does a *cortical* organoid of human origin approach the computational capabilities of a similar-sized piece of *human cortex*? I wish the answer was "really close". Unfortunately, it's more like "not even close!" but I believe that it's quite promising if we keep at it for a bit.
